The town

Bournemouth is three economies sharing a postcode.

There's the visitor economy — hotels, restaurants, attractions and everything that lives or dies by the summer. There's a substantial professional and financial services presence, much of it back-office operations for national firms. And there's a creative and digital cluster fed by two universities, one of them an arts university, which means an unusual concentration of design and film graduates for a town this size.

Those three groups need very different things from content. A seafront restaurant needs volume and speed through the season. A financial services firm needs credibility and restraint. An agency or studio needs to look better than its competitors at a glance, because that is the pitch.

We work with all three, which is mostly a matter of knowing which one you are before proposing anything.

Seasonality

The mistake almost every seasonal business makes with content.

Producing content in August, when nobody has a spare minute.

If your trade is seasonal, your content calendar has to run ahead of it, not alongside it. The summer is when you should be spending a content library, not building one — and the businesses that struggle are almost always the ones trying to shoot, write and post during their busiest twelve weeks.

We plan and shoot in the shoulder months. February and October are quiet, staff have time, and a single well-planned day produces enough material to run the channels right through the season. It costs less, it looks better, and nobody is being asked to write captions during a Saturday service.

For non-seasonal businesses the same logic applies to your own cycle — batch ahead of the busy period, whenever yours falls.

Five years of this work. One year of this name.

The studio has been making content for businesses since 2020 — strategy, film, photography, websites and the search work that makes them findable. It operated under a different name for most of that time, and became Nexra Creative in 2026.

We mention it because the distinction matters when you're deciding whether to trust a studio. The brand is new. The work behind it isn't, and neither is the judgement that comes from five years of finding out which approaches hold up and which quietly don't.

Why we exist

There is a gap between the freelancer who disappears and the agency that sends an account manager.

Big agencies solve the consistency problem by adding people — a strategist, a planner, a junior, an account director, and a monthly invoice that reflects all of them. Freelancers solve the cost problem but rarely the consistency one. Most businesses sit uncomfortably between the two, paying for a lot of process or getting brilliant work at unpredictable intervals.

We built the studio to sit in that gap. Small enough that the person who plans your content is the person who makes it. Structured enough that it arrives on time every month, whether or not anyone feels inspired that week.

That's the whole proposition. It isn't complicated, but very few people do it consistently.

Process

Six steps. The same six every time, from first email to monthly report.

How the work happens

  • First replyWithin one working day
  • First callThirty minutes, no deck
  • PricingFixed, in writing, before anything starts
  • ReportingMonthly, in plain English
  1. Enquiry. You get in touch with a rough idea of what you're trying to change. We read it properly and reply within one working day — a real reply, not an acknowledgement.
  2. Conversation. Thirty minutes, no deck. What the business does, what's working, what you actually want to be different. If we're not the right people, we'll say so here rather than three weeks later.
  3. Proposal. A short written document: scope, deliverables, timeline and a fixed price. Short enough to read in one sitting, specific enough to hold us to. No day rates that drift.
  4. Agreement. We settle exactly what gets produced before anything starts — how many pieces, in what formats, to what schedule. Both sides know what "done" looks like on day one.
  5. Production. The shoot, the build, the writing. You see work in progress rather than a grand reveal at the end, because the grand reveal is how projects go wrong.
  6. Publish & report. Everything goes out on the agreed schedule, approved in advance. Once a month, in plain English: what we made, what happened, what we'd change — including the parts that didn't work.

Fit

We'd rather be honest about this up front than three months in.

Good fit

  • You have something genuinely interesting to show — a craft, a product, an archive, real expertise
  • You want fewer, better things rather than maximum output
  • You can give feedback quickly and make decisions without a committee
  • You're thinking in years, not weeks

Probably not

  • You need forty posts a month and the cheapest possible rate
  • Success is defined purely by follower count
  • You want guaranteed rankings by a fixed date
  • The work needs to go through five rounds of approval before anything ships

None of that is a judgement — those are real requirements and there are studios built to meet them well. We're just not one of them.
